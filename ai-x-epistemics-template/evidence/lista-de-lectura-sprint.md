# Sprint reading list, as used in this report

Saved: 2026-11-15.

Source: the Resources tab of the sprint page, https://apartresearch.com/sprints/ai-x-epistemics-research-sprint-2026-11-13-to-2026-11-15 , read on 15 November 2026. The entries below give each work's title, authors, venue and year as that page lists them. The one-line descriptions are this team's paraphrase of the page. The team did not read these papers in full during the sprint, and the report says so.

## Works named in the report

- SycEval. Fanous et al., AIES 2025. Listed for answer-flipping under rebuttal pressure.
- ELEPHANT. Cheng et al., 2025. Listed for social sycophancy.
- The MASK Benchmark. Ren et al., CAIS, 2025. Listed for models contradicting their own beliefs under pressure.
- DarkBench. Kran et al., ICLR 2025. Listed for manipulative patterns in model replies.
- Sycophantic AI Decreases Prosocial Intentions and Promotes Dependence. Cheng et al., Science, 2025. Listed for the effect of agreeable chatbots on users.
- Sycophancy in GPT-4o. OpenAI, 2025. Listed as a first-party postmortem.
- Design Sketches: Angels-on-the-Shoulder. Cotton-Barratt, Vaintrob, Sourbut and Hadshar, Forethought, 2026. Listed as the menu for the epistemic-products track, including deep briefings before a decision.
- AI for AI for Epistemics. Cotton-Barratt and Finnveden, Forethought, 2026. Listed for the point that early epistemic tools gain trust whether or not they earn it.

## Example projects named on the same page

The page lists example projects per track. Three are close to this study, and the study is none of them.

- FlipRate, track 1: how a model's answer changes across escalating rebuttals, separating a warranted update from capitulation.
- Decision-Brief Rubric, track 3: a rubric from domain expertise, and a harness that scores chatbot briefings against it.
- Provenance Study, track 2: the same tasks with and without machine-readable trust signals, to see whether behaviour improves.
